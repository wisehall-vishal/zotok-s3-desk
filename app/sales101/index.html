Mindset

Four shifts before any tactic.

07 / 48

Mindset 1 · Where you start

Sell on what they have, not on what we have.

DON’T

Open with us. “200+ deployments, agentic AI, SAP integration, Meta partner…”

He’s nodding. He’s also checking his phone.

DO

Open with him. “Your distributors send orders on WhatsApp at 10 pm. Who reads them? What happens to the ones nobody reads?”

FROM OUR CALLS

KRBL and others

Almost every demo we reviewed opened the same way: product overview, dealer menu, AI bot. At KRBL, the prospect had to connect the dots to his world himself.

ZoTok Sales 101 · Don’t do this. Do that.

08 / 48

Mindset 2 · What moves people

Talk about the bleed, not the brochure.

DON’T

“With ZoTok your channel becomes digital, engaged and future-ready.”

People smile at upside. Then they wait for a better quarter.

DO

“Right now, how many retailer orders slip through your WhatsApp groups every week? What does one missed order cost?”

People act on pain. And they act now to stop a bleed.

FROM OUR CALLS

Kidlen India

The owner wasn’t buying AI. He was missing orders every day and couldn’t track fulfilment. He bought an end to that bleed.

Evidence: loss aversion. A loss hurts about twice as much as an equal gain feels good (Kahneman & Tversky)

09 / 48

Mindset 3 · How much to say

They buy trust, not details.

DON’T

Walk him through all 40 features, every integration, every edge case.

Each detail is a new question. Enough questions become “Let me think about it.”

DO

Show the one workflow that fixes his pain. Answer what he asks, crisply.

Keep the rest in your pocket for the next meeting.

FROM OUR CALLS

Greenply

Thirty minutes, eight modules: handwritten orders to SFTP to loyalty. The IT head followed all of it, then said: “Give us a couple of weeks.”

ZoTok Sales 101 · Don’t do this. Do that.

10 / 48

Mindset 4 · The demo

Don’t demo the product. Demo his Tuesday.

DON’T

Full product tour on a demo account: dummy brand, dummy SKUs, every module.

He watches our software. He can’t see his business in it.

DO

His brand, his five fastest SKUs, one of his real distributors’ names. Three moments, no more.

Tell, show, tell. Then ask: “Where would this break for you?”

FROM OUR CALLS

Across our demos

What lit up room after room: WhatsApp group intelligence, and a handwritten order becoming a clean one. At Greenply, a live plywood distributor beat any slide.

Discovery in practice

Don’t fill a form. Follow the pain.

DON’T

“How many distributors? Which ERP? How many SKUs? Monthly order volume?”

That’s a survey. He’s bored by question three.

DO

“Walk me through what happens when a retailer orders at 9 pm.”

Then: “And when that goes wrong, what does it cost you?” Get the facts inside the story.

18 / 48

Crime 01 · Talking too much

He doesn’t care how much you know.

DON’T

Forty-minute pitch, ten minutes of Q&A. You leave feeling brilliant.

He leaves with no decision.

DO

Your job is to get him to a decision, not to impress him.

Ask, listen, play his words back. Talking more than him? Stop and ask a question.

FROM OUR CALLS

Tata Motors

Forty minutes of nodding: “Very wonderfully put.” “I totally agree.” Then: “Let the thoughts sink in.” Nodding is not buying.

Evidence: Gong Labs, 326,000 calls. Reps talked 57% of the time on won deals, 62% on lost ones

19 / 48

Crime 02 · Sending the price

Never let the price travel without you.

DON’T

“Can you send the pricing?” “Sure sir, will mail it today.”

The PDF lands with no context. He compares a number, not a solution.

DO

“Happy to. It depends on two or three things about your setup. Twenty minutes on Thursday? I’ll walk you through it.”

Open the price in the room, where you can hear the reaction.

FROM OUR CALLS

Tata Motors

Our close: “I’ll send a solution deck, screenshots and indicative pricing.” Reply: “Sure, send it across.” No meeting booked. The price went alone.

ZoTok Sales 101 · Don’t do this. Do that.

20 / 48

Crime 03 · Blinking first

Silence is not a request for a discount.

DON’T

You say the number. He goes quiet. Panic: “But we can do something for you…”

He learns silence pays. He’ll use it every time.

DO

Say the number. Then stop. Count five seconds in your head.

Let him justify it, or tell you his budget. Either way, you learn something.

Crime 05 · The price alibi

Lost on price? Almost never.

DON’T

“We lost, sir. Price was high.” Case closed.

Nothing learned. Same mistake next month.

DO

Ask what really happened. Did he feel the pain? Did we meet the person who signs? Was there a reason to act now?

Price is usually the polite excuse for a missing yes.

The pattern

An objection is a question in disguise.

1

Acknowledge

“I hear you.” No arguing, no defending.

2

Ask

One question that gets to the real concern.

3

Shut up

Let him answer. The real objection comes next.

Objection 2 · Stalling

HE SAYS

“Let me think about it.”

DON’T

“Sure sir, take your time.” (Translation: goodbye.)

The best follow-up is the one you don’t need

The rule of three

Three follow-ups. Then stop.

#1 · GIVE VALUE

Saw how another [category] brand cut missed retailer orders on WhatsApp. Thought of your distributor calls. No reply needed.

Something useful, no ask attached.

#2 · EASY YES OR NO

Shall I hold 20 mins on Thursday at 4 pm to walk your team through it? A no is completely fine too.

A specific timebox he can answer in one word.

#3 · CLOSE THE DOOR

I’ll stop nudging after this one. If fixing [his problem] becomes a priority, I’m one message away.

Polite, final, and the door stays open.

AC2 · Enterprise, ₹2,000 Cr+

One champion is a single point of failure.

DON’T

Everything runs through one friendly sales head.

He changes roles, and the deal quietly dies with him.

DO

Map the room: Sales (pain), IT / CIO (SAP, security, Meta compliance), Finance (ROI), Procurement (price). Meet each one.

Give your champion a business case he can forward.

India lens

Five rules of the road.

“Will revert” is a maybe.

Put a date on it before you hang up: “Shall I call you Friday at 11?”

“Best price?” is a ritual.

Not an objection. Walk in with your give-get list ready.

Know the calendar.

March closes budgets, April opens them. In festive season his team is heads-down, so aim to go live before it.

Chai builds trust. Dates build pipeline.

Relationship first, but never relationship only. Every warm meeting still ends with a next step.

WhatsApp is his office too.

Message before you call. Keep it short. Match his language. Respect his evenings.
